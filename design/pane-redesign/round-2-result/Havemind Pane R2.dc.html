Round 2 — the tabbed pane, tightened

Your mitigation is the right one: an alarm outranks the strip, so the strip is free to be a signpost. Everything below accepts tabs as settled and only argues about the chrome above the content. 2a is what shipped, redrawn honestly at 300px and 250px so the rest has a baseline to beat.

One number to hold onto: at 300px the shipped chrome is 34px header + 40px action bar + 44px tab strip = 118px before a single word of content. That is a quarter of a typical pane's height spent on navigation for a four-destination product.

Answers

  1. Fold the action bar into the header. Two rows of icons is one row too many, and Obsidian already has a place for view actions: the view header. Moving them there costs 0px of new chrome, leaves the tab strip as the only row of targets, and removes the read of "eight equal buttons" — header icons are visibly chrome, tabs are visibly navigation. That is 2b, and it is what I would ship. 2c goes further and deletes the row entirely by pushing each action into the tab that owns it; sparer, but two of the four actions get harder to find.
  2. Labels, one line, no icons — and let the pane pick below 250px. Icon-over-label spends 44px to say what 26px of text says better, and it is the layout that truncates first, because the label gets the narrowest box in the strip. Single-line labels fit four tabs down to ~250px. Below that, icon-only with title + aria-label. It is a container width rule, not a taste call — see the ladder in 2d, and drag Pane width in Tweaks on 2g to watch it switch.
  3. Modal, launched from People. Invite-as-tab has a defect the round-1 objection did not cover: the strip changes shape by role. Owners get four tabs, guests get three, and the pane becomes two structurally different products to describe, screenshot and support. A momentary task is also holding a permanent slot in the most valuable strip of pixels in the plugin. Put it where "who is in this vault" already lives — a row at the foot of People — and let the pending device announce itself above the strip like any other alarm. 2e.
  4. One line. Four lines explaining that nothing has happened is the pane talking about itself. Waiting for the other device · 4:52 with the spinner says the same thing and leaves the space for the device that is about to arrive — which is when the screen has something to say. 2f has both, and the one case where I would keep prose: the very first invitation a user ever sends.
  5. Cut list below. Net effect: chrome drops from 118px to 60px, targets on first paint from eight to four, and the strip stops carrying numbers nobody can act on. 2g is the pane with every cut applied, light and dark, live-resizable.

What I would cut

  • The action bar as a row. Four icons into the header (2b).
  • The Invite tab. Modal from People, so the strip is the same three tabs for everyone (2e).
  • Sync now. Sync is automatic; a manual button implies it might not be. It belongs in the overflow menu, and Retry now already appears on the status line in the states where pushing something actually helps.
  • Icons in the tab strip. They cost a line of height and are not distinctive at 15px — an activity squiggle and a users glyph do not read differently at a glance.
  • Counts on Activity and People. "12 today" and "2" are numbers you cannot act on, and they train the eye to skip the one count that matters. Keep the red conflict count; drop the rest.
  • "Show all 12" inside the Activity tab. The tab is the full list now — it scrolls. That button is a leftover from a collapsed section.
  • The waiting paragraph (2f), and the help icon in the connected state — a person who has a working vault is not looking for a tutorial. Keep help in the pre-connect screens and in overflow.

Assumptions

  • The alarm zone above the strip is never collapsible and never scrolls away — it is the one thing in the pane with a fixed position.
  • Header action icons keep aria-label and Obsidian tooltips; the authorship toggle keeps aria-pressed and shows state by fill, not by colour alone.
  • Roving tabindex stays as built; the alarm zone sits before the tablist in DOM order, so a screen reader meets the conflict before the navigation.
  • 250px is the practical floor, not a hard one — the ladder degrades below it rather than breaking.

ALARM ON A NON-STATUS TAB — the conflict block sits between header and strip, so it is present on Activity too, and it precedes the tablist in DOM order.

Why the header and not the tabs. An action and a destination are different verbs. Tabs answer "where am I looking", actions answer "do a thing now" — merging them means a click either navigates or fires a side effect depending on which cell you hit, which is the one thing a tab strip must never do.

The header is also where Obsidian users already look for view actions, and it has spare width: "Havemind" plus three 26px buttons fits to 214px before the title starts eliding.

Cost: sync-now and help lose their one-click access. I think that is correct — see the cut list — but if help must stay visible, it is the fourth header icon and still costs nothing.

<250px — icon only, tooltip + aria-label. A word truncated to "Activi…" is worse than a glyph you can hover.

The rule. One ResizeObserver on the pane, three classes: ≥330 → icon+label, 250–329 → label, <250 → icon. Nothing else in the pane responds to width, and the labels never truncate at any size, because the layout changes before they have to.

Why not always icon-only. Three tabs is inside the range where words are cheap and a glyph vocabulary has to be learned. Icon-only is a fallback, not a target.

Why the count survives every step. The red conflict number is not a label, it renders next to the icon in icon-only mode, and the alarm above the strip is doing the real work regardless.

If you only take one thing from Q2: the shipped icon-over-label layout is the only one of the three that breaks, because it gives the words the narrowest possible box and then centres them. Dropping to labels at 300px is a one-line change and buys 14px of height back.

PENDING DEVICE — the one invitation state that outranks a tab. Accent, not red: it is expected and good, but it is time-boxed and needs a human. Same slot as a conflict, never both invented twice.

The argument you did not make in round 1. Invite-as-tab means the strip has four cells for owners and three for guests. Two shapes of the same pane is a documentation, screenshot and support cost forever, and a guest who reads about "the Invite tab" cannot find it.

Where it goes instead. Header icon (always reachable, owner-only, no layout change when absent) plus a row at the foot of People. Two doors, both where a person already is when they think "add someone".

What stays in the pane. Only the approval — because it is time-limited and interactive. It renders in the alarm slot above the strip, in accent rather than red, so the vocabulary is "something needs you" without claiming something is wrong.
